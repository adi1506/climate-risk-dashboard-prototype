# Extreme heat benchmarks for Nimbus Cement

The dashboard's **Heat Priority Score** combines the frequency of days at or above 40°C daily maximum air temperature with the scale of the workforce and production at each plant. [Nimbus Heat Priority Score method](./nimbus-hot-days-2026-2040.md)

The companion comparison uses **modelled outdoor peak wet-bulb globe temperature (WBGT)** to screen for humid heat, solar load and wind, which matter for exposed quarry, yard and loading work. FEWS NET uses days at or above 30°C WBGT, and Endeavour Mining uses days above 32°C WBGT among its screening indicators. Nimbus calculates both day counts and ranks plants by cumulative annual exceedance above 30°C WBGT. [FEWS NET Heat Exposure Projections](https://fews.net/heat-exposure-projections) · [Endeavour Mining Annual Report 2025, p. 55](https://edv-14806-s3.s3.eu-west-2.amazonaws.com/files/9317/7265/6400/EDV_-_2025_Annual_Report.pdf) · [Nimbus 2026–2040 WBGT assessment](./nimbus-extreme-heat-2026-2040.md)

## Why extreme heat is particularly relevant to cement manufacturing

Cement plants combine outdoor work in quarries, yards and loading areas with processes that generate heat. A NIOSH field investigation at Lehigh Portland Cement measured about 31–39°C WBGT while kiln assistants cleaned a preheater. Investigators documented a substantial radiant heat load and protective clothing that impeded sweat evaporation. The outdoor temperature screen helps identify plants for review, and measurements at the work location capture the additional heat from equipment and tasks. [NIOSH field investigation at Lehigh Portland Cement](https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/171584) · [UK Environment Agency, Summer daily maximum temperature, impacts 1 and 3](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/adapting-to-climate-change-industry-sector-examples-for-your-risk-assessment/cement-lime-and-minerals-examples-for-your-adapting-to-climate-change-risk-assessment#summer-daily-maximum-temperature)

### Why the Heat Priority Score uses workforce, cement and clinker

| FY2025–26 input | Why it is useful for the Nimbus screen | What a plant review needs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Reported employee headcount** | Approximates the scale of the workforce to consider when reviewing heat illness and changes in work schedules. A larger site can have more people to protect under similar heat, work and staffing conditions. [Nimbus plant inventory](../nimbus_cement_plants.csv) · [WHO/WMO workplace heat guidance](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240099814) | Identify who works in exposed areas by role and shift, including contractors, and measure work-area WBGT with workload, clothing and radiant process heat accounted for. [NIOSH occupational heat criteria, chapter 5](https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/docs/2016-106/pdfs/2016-106.pdf) |
| **Finished cement output** | Measures the scale of final production that relies on continuity of grinding, material handling and dispatch at both integrated works and grinding units. At equal fractional disruption, a higher-throughput site would have more finished tonnes affected. [Nimbus plant inventory](../nimbus_cement_plants.csv) · [European Commission JRC cement reference, §§1.2.6–1.2.7, pp. 38–42](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/bitstream/JRC83006/clm_published_def.pdf) · [Holcim 2024 Climate Report, p. 73](https://www.holcim.com/sites/holcim/files/docs/28022025-holcim-climate-report-2024.pdf) | Check operating logs, equipment limits, storage buffers and whether production and dispatch can recover after a hot shift. [UK Environment Agency cement-sector guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/adapting-to-climate-change-industry-sector-examples-for-your-risk-assessment/cement-lime-and-minerals-examples-for-your-adapting-to-climate-change-risk-assessment#summer-daily-maximum-temperature) |
| **Clinker produced on site** | Measures the scale of the kiln and clinker cooling stage at integrated works. The cooler recovers heat for the kiln system and brings hot clinker down to a temperature suitable for downstream equipment. At a grinding unit, zero records the absence of local clinker production. The unit can still depend on incoming clinker. [Nimbus plant inventory](../nimbus_cement_plants.csv) · [European Commission JRC cement reference, §1.2.5.7, pp. 35–37, and §1.2.6, pp. 38–39](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/bitstream/JRC83006/clm_published_def.pdf) | Check kiln and cooler performance against ambient conditions, then review clinker stock and alternative supply. Chittorgarh shipped 1.12 Mt to Mundra, and Baloda Bazar shipped 1.28 Mt to Paradip and Durgapur. [Nimbus plant inventory](../nimbus_cement_plants.csv) |

The three inputs indicate **scale for screening**. Weights in the Heat Priority Score express Nimbus's follow-up priorities. Estimating heat illness, equipment deterioration, downtime, tonnes lost or operating expense would require site-specific exposure and response data. Keep cement and clinker as separate components because clinker can already be contained in the finished cement tonnage. Use the recorded clinker shipments to interpret downstream dependencies. ## Apply the WBGT metric to the 2040 Comparison

1. **Use the same method at both horizons.** For each Assessment Portfolio coordinate, use the same climate models and WBGT threshold in both windows. Calculate each model's annual exposure, then average across years in each window and compare ensemble medians and paired changes. [Nimbus 2026–2040 heat assessment](./nimbus-extreme-heat-2026-2040.md) · [CarbonPlan methods](https://carbonplan.org/research/extreme-heat-explainer)
2. **Check coverage at the supplied coordinates.** CarbonPlan publishes daily shade and sun WBGT approximations for roughly 24,000 climate regions and 15,300 urban centres, with 26 global models and SSP2-4.5. The sun estimate uses a daily solar radiation and wind adjustment. Its listed daily stores cover 1985–2014, 2020–2039 and 2040–2059. All nine Nimbus coordinates fall within named urban-centre polygons. The matched areas support calculating the 2020–2032 and 2034–2046 windows directly from daily data. UCSB's 0.05° CHC-CMIP6 product offers temperature-and-humidity-derived daily maximum WBGT for a historical period and 2030/2050 time slices. [CarbonPlan data and methods](https://github.com/carbonplan/extreme-heat#data) · [Nimbus plant matches](./nimbus-extreme-heat/plant-rankings.csv) · [UCSB Climate Hazards Center, CHC-CMIP6](https://www.chc.ucsb.edu/data/chc-cmip6)
3. **Use task-level measurements for workplace decisions.** Measure WBGT at the actual work locations for exposed quarry work, dispatch, maintenance and kiln-side tasks. Account for metabolic workload, acclimatisation, clothing and PPE, ventilation, radiant process heat and work-rest schedules. NIOSH Table 5-1 lists 28°C WBGT for acclimatised workers doing moderate work and 26°C for heavy work in its comparison table, illustrating why a single climate-screening threshold does not establish a site work limit. For equipment, use local air temperature and plant-specific derating or maintenance triggers for relevant systems such as bearings and conveyors. [WHO/WMO workplace heat guidance, 22 August 2025](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240099814) · [NIOSH 2016-106, pp. 3–4 and table 5-1](https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/docs/2016-106/pdfs/2016-106.pdf) · [UK Environment Agency cement-sector guidance, updated 17 May 2023](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/adapting-to-climate-change-industry-sector-examples-for-your-risk-assessment/cement-lime-and-minerals-examples-for-your-adapting-to-climate-change-risk-assessment#summer-daily-maximum-temperature)

Where only temperature and paired humidity are available, **heat-index exceedance days** give a shade-based fallback. Where only daily maximum temperature is available, Nimbus's existing **degree-days above 40°C** capture the size of exceedances as well as their frequency. Consecutive exceedance days indicate sustained events, and warm nights from daily minimum temperature can flag reduced overnight cooling for housing and night shifts. IMD's official heatwave criteria also depend on local conditions. Its FAQ specifies a lower minimum temperature and a departure test for coastal heatwaves. [NOAA heat-index equation](https://www.wpc.ncep.noaa.gov/html/heatindex_equation.shtml) · [IMD heatwave FAQ, pp. 1–2](https://mausam.imd.gov.in/pdfs/heatcolduser/FAQ_heat_wave.pdf) · [WHO/WMO heatwave guidance](https://www.who.int/publications/m/item/heatwaves-and-health--guidance-on-warning-system-development)
