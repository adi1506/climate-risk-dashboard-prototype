# Carbon price benchmarks for Nimbus Cement

Reviewed 23 November 2026. The discovery brief selected **₹1,000 per tonne of direct CO₂** as a round-number starting point for Carbon Price Sensitivity. A Cement Manufacturers’ Association (CMA) publication illustrates Indian cement compliance exposure at an assumed **US$10/tCO₂e**, about **₹955/tCO₂e** at the October 2026 average exchange rate. EY-Parthenon independently used **US$10/tCO₂** as the starting point in its 2026 central scenario for Indian cement and tested other starting prices of US$5 and US$15. These assumptions support the order of magnitude for a low-price sensitivity. ## How cement companies set and use internal prices

An internal shadow price assigns a monetary value to emissions in planning. One application is to multiply a project’s expected emissions savings versus its baseline by the assumed price and include that benefit in the investment appraisal. For example, a project that avoids 10,000 tonnes of CO₂ annually has an illustrative annual carbon benefit of ₹2.7 crore at ₹2,700/tCO₂. Companies can compare that benefit alongside equipment and operating costs. ## How India’s compliance scheme works

The Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change’s Greenhouse Gases Emission Intensity Target Rules set facility-specific targets for listed cement units, in tonnes of CO₂e per tonne of equivalent product. For a facility above its target, the gross certificate shortfall equals **(verified actual intensity − target intensity) × actual equivalent output**. A facility below target can receive certificates based on the reverse difference. Eligible banked certificates can help meet future obligations. The compliance calculation accounts for emissions within the applicable facility boundary, which can include purchased electricity and heat. Estimating Nimbus’s cash exposure would require the listed entity and applicable target, verified intensity and equivalent output, eligible certificate balances, and a certificate price. [MoEFCC Target Rules, rule 4](https://beeindia.gov.in/WriteReadData/L45218/1663540214634949.pdf#page=30) · [MoEFCC Target Rules, rule 5 and following schedules](https://beeindia.gov.in/WriteReadData/L45218/1663540214634949.pdf#page=31) · [BEE Detailed Procedure for Compliance Mechanism, §§5–6 and 8–10](https://beeindia.gov.in/sites/default/files/2024-07/Detailed%20Procedure%20for%20Compliance%20Procedure%20under%20CCTS.pdf)

For example, a facility that produces 1 million tonnes of equivalent product and exceeds its target by 0.01 tCO₂e per tonne has a gross shortfall of 10,000 Carbon Credit Certificates (CCCs). That would be ₹1 crore at a hypothetical ₹1,000 per CCC if it had to buy the entire shortfall. Shree separately reported a potential ₹7.05 crore annual CCTS exposure from credit purchases and/or emissions offsetting in FY2025–26. The passage gives the exposure amount and risk description, with no certificate price or shortfall volume specified. [MoEFCC Target Rules, rule 5](https://beeindia.gov.in/WriteReadData/L45218/1663540214634949.pdf#page=31) · [Shree ESG Data Book FY2025–26, p. 21](https://www.shreecement.com/uploads/cleanupload/ESGDataBookFY202526.pdf#page=21)

**Market status as of 23 November 2026.** Under Central Electricity Regulatory Commission (CERC) regulation 11, one CCC represents one tonne of CO₂e. The exchange or another permitted venue discovers the price within a floor and ceiling that CERC approves on a BEE proposal. BEE published a draft transaction procedure for consultation on 19 October. CERC’s 23 October hearing record lists 12 January 2027 as the next hearing date for the three exchange product petitions. The sources reviewed yielded no approved numeric price band or published clearing price for compliance CCCs. [CERC CCC Trading Regulations, regulation 11](https://cercind.gov.in/regulations/GZ-205.pdf#page=9) · [BEE consultation notice](https://www.beeindia.gov.in/WriteReadData/RTF1984/RTF-PDF-20510f1d3ee6d4ba_1792664827.pdf) · [CERC hearing record, p. 2](https://cercind.gov.in/2026/ROP/453-MP-2026-Ors_231026.pdf#page=2) · [World Bank Carbon Pricing Dashboard, India](https://carbonpricingdashboard.worldbank.org/compliance-factsheet/ETS_IN)

## Implications for the dashboard

Use **₹1,000/tCO₂ in the 2026 scenario** and **₹2,700/tCO₂ in the 2040 scenario** for the fixed FY2025–26 portfolio. The EY-Parthenon normative scenario starts at US$10/tCO₂ in 2026. Its projection uses the mean of the six EU carbon-price growth rates on page 19 (about 7.64% annually). Compounding for 14 years gives about US$28.04/tCO₂, consistent with the pink 2040 marker. Converting both nominal dollar values at the October 2026 average of ₹95.5063 per dollar gives ₹955.06 and roughly ₹2,678.26 per tonne, rounded to the nearest ₹100.
